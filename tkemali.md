## Reine-Claude tkemali

For one small jar:

* 500 g Reine-Claude plums
* 40–60 ml water
* 2–3 garlic cloves
* ½ small bunch fresh coriander
* ¼ small bunch fresh dill
* 8–10 fresh mint leaves
* ½–1 small green chilli, or chilli flakes
* ¾ teaspoon ground coriander or crushed coriander seeds
* ½ teaspoon **Svan salt**, then more to taste
* 1–2 tablespoons lemon juice or white-wine vinegar
* Optional: a pinch of black pepper

### Method

1. **Taste the plums first.**
   Firm, slightly underripe Reine-Claude plums are ideal. If they taste very sweet, expect to use closer to 2 tablespoons of lemon juice or vinegar.

2. **Cook the plums.**
   Halve and stone them. Put them in a saucepan with 40 ml water, cover and simmer for approximately 10–15 minutes, until completely soft.

3. **Purée them.**
   Blend until smooth. For a more traditional, silky consistency, press the cooked plums through a sieve instead. Traditional recipes often boil the plums whole and then sieve away the stones and skins. ([Georgian Recipes][1])

4. **Add the seasoning.**
   Return the purée to low heat. Add the coriander, Svan salt and chilli. Simmer uncovered for around 5 minutes.

5. **Add the fresh aromatics.**
   Finely chop or blend the garlic, coriander leaves, dill and mint with a spoonful of the plum purée. Stir this mixture into the saucepan and cook gently for another 2–3 minutes.

6. **Correct the flavour.**
   Add lemon juice or vinegar gradually. The finished sauce should taste **decisively sour, savoury, herbal and garlicky—not like sweet plum chutney**. Add more Svan salt only after the acidity is balanced.

7. **Cool and refrigerate.**
   It will thicken slightly once cold. Add a splash of water if it becomes too thick.

### About your spice blends

Use **Svan salt carefully**, because it already supplies salt, garlic and Georgian aromatics. I would **not add the kharcho blend** to tkemali; it can make the sauce muddy and stew-like. At most, add a very small pinch if your blend is mainly coriander and blue fenugreek.

Serve it cold or at room temperature with roasted chicken, fish, prawns, potatoes, aubergine or fried eggs. Other plum varieties can be used for tkemali, but sweetness and acidity need to be adjusted accordingly. ([georgia.to][2])
